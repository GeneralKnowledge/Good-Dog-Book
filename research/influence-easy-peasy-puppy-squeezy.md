## Mann’s training philosophy (paraphrase)

Public summaries and Mann’s own interviews emphasise:

1. **Force-free, reward-based** training — teach what you want; avoid punishment-based “quick fixes.”  
2. **How the dog feels before what the dog does** — safety, optimism, and bond first; behaviours follow when the emotional baseline is sound.  
3. **Environment control** — prevent mistakes (legal chews, supervision, gates) rather than yelling after the fact.  
4. **Cue → mark (“good”) → reinforce** — same shape as our teaching loop (Chapters 3–4).  
5. **Short, fun sessions** — fits RSPCA short-session guidance we already cite.  
6. **Three Ds** — **distance, distraction, duration** — change **one** at a time when proofing (our Chapter 5 §2).  
7. **Mutually exclusive behaviours (MEBs)** — teach a behaviour that cannot happen at the same time as the problem (e.g. hold toy instead of bark at door; eye contact before “go play” instead of pulling).  
8. **Core owner skills** — sit, recall, loose-lead walking, engagement/eye contact — matches our **basics-first** spine (sit, come, walk nicely).
