# Book proposition

**Status:** Living document (Stage B).  
**Language:** British English.

## Working title

*Good Dog* (working title — display title subject to author approval)

## One-line pitch

A definitive, practical guide that teaches ordinary owners how dogs learn, how to train with kindness and clarity, and how to know when management or professional help is the right move — written so you actually want to read it.

## Central argument

Training works best when owners **set the dog up to succeed**, **reward what they want**, **notice discomfort early**, and **match difficulty to the real world** — not when they hunt for a single trick or dominate a frightened animal. Management, veterinary care, and qualified behaviour support are part of good training, not admissions of failure.

## Intended reader

**Primary:** Ordinary dog owners who want to raise or live successfully with a dog and are not professional trainers. Accessible to first-time owners; still useful to experienced owners who want clearer behaviour literacy and better troubleshooting.

**Life stages:** Puppies, adolescents, adults, and rescue dogs — with explicit notes that history, health, and temperament differ.

## Practical promise

After reading, a motivated owner should be able to:

- Explain, in plain language, how reward-based learning works  
- Run a repeatable teaching loop (setup → cue → mark/reward → release → generalise)  
- Choose rewards, timing, and session length sensibly  
- Teach foundations: engagement, settle, recall basics, comfortable lead walking, handling, home manners  
- Read stress signals with appropriate uncertainty (context, not single-cue diagnosis)  
- Respond to setbacks without blame or escalation  
- Recognise when to manage, call the vet, or seek a qualified reward-based professional  

## Voice and tone

- Warm, witty, conversational, memorable  
- Intelligent without academic fog; practical without dullness  
- Compassionate without sentimentality or preaching  
- Serious about welfare; not solemn about ourselves  

**Humour:** Recognisable owner experiences, precise observation, understatement, everyday absurdity with a dog in the house. Never the dog as butt of cruelty; never mock distressed owners; never trivialise fear, pain, aggression, or welfare.

**Originality:** Develop an authorial voice for this book. Do not imitate another writer’s distinctive prose.

## Training and welfare position

- Reward-based training and welfare-centred handling as the default  
- No pain, intimidation, forced exposure, or “dominance/submission” framing as training method  
- No implication that reward-based training makes every problem easy or fast  
- Distinguish training, management, environmental change, veterinary input, and specialist behaviour support  
- Avoid universal guarantees; explain individual factors and boundaries  

## Editorial ambition

More than a manual: a coherent reading journey with a clear through-line, realistic scenarios, actionable steps, observable success criteria, troubleshooting for common failure modes, minimal jargon (introduced in context), and minimal repetition across chapters.

## Relationship to Good Dog (app)

The book may share **ideas and carefully adapted material** with the Good Dog app but is a **standalone work**. Optional companion mention only after author approval (see `scope.md`).

## Not claimed

- Professional accreditation, IMDT endorsement, or veterinary approval unless a documented review exists  
- That all content has been independently verified — see evidence policy and open questions
