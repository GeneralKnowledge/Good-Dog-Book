## Opening

The door is a portal. For dogs it often means **arousal**: footsteps, voices, smells of other people’s coats. Most “bad manners” at the threshold are predictable — and fixable first with **management**, not with forcing face-to-face hellos until the dog is ready. If your dog stiffens, barks, or backs away when guests appear, read Chapter 12 first. Comfort beats politeness.

## Section 1 — Doors: safety before politeness

The door is not a test of rank. It is a **safety pinch point**: darting into traffic, bolting past children, rehearsing excitement every time the bell rings. Teach manners **after** you prevent rehearsal of disaster.

**Management defaults:** baby gate or second barrier; lead on before the handle turns; keys and parcels staged so you are not juggling while the dog squeezes through. For excitable dogs, **separate room** with a stuffed Kong beats “sit stay” in the blast zone while you admit the courier.

### Train yourself

- **Know:** Every uncontrolled exit teaches “doors explode with freedom.” Your hurry is the enemy.  
- **Do:** One routine — dog behind gate or on lead, you open, you release only when calm enough; practise with nobody there first.  
- **Don't:** Open wide “just once” while the dog rockets past; shout sit from the doorstep while the dog is already gone.

### Train the dog

- **Know:** Door sounds predict chaos or predict calm — your setup picks which.  
- **Do:** Learn wait behind barrier, or go to mat when bell rings, at sub-threshold excitement first.  
- **Don't:** Learn that bolting works because someone always chases.

## Section 2 — Greetings and visitors

**Sources:** Dogs Trust visitor training (`book-research-src-dt-visitor-training`, verified 2026-10-18) plus threshold management when the dog is worried.

**Goal:** Calm arrivals — not forced face-to-face hellos. If the dog is over threshold, **abort** (distance, guest leaves, try another day) even if you have not finished the polite script.

**Default setup:**

1. **Lead or secure room before the door opens.** Dog cannot rehearse jumping, bolting, or snapping at the gap. Baby gate or behind a door is fine — management is smart.  
2. **Distance is non-negotiable.** Guest stays outside or in hallway; dog sees or smells at a distance where body language stays **loose enough to eat** (if food-motivated). If they stiffen, whale-eye, or bark tight — **abort**: guest steps back or you move the dog further away.  
3. **One calm helper optional.** If you practise at all, one adult who follows your script beats a party of enthusiastic greeters.  
4. **Exit on stiffening.** Stiffening is data. End the approach; pay calm at distance; try another day.  
5. **Pay the dog for **choice** behaviours you want at distance — quiet four paws, look at you, sniff mat — not for lunging toward the stranger.

**Scripts for humans (boring on purpose):**

- Guest: “I won’t touch until you say; I’ll sit where you point.”  
- You: “If he’s soft, you may toss a treat **past** him, not at his face. If not, we chat through the gate.”

No child carriers, no leaning over, no “dogs love me” exceptions.

### Train yourself

- **Know:** A polite visitor experience for **humans** may be the wrong goal for **this dog today**; distance protects everyone’s face.  
- **Do:** Plan greetings like fire drills — where dog goes, where guest stands, how you end early.  
- **Don't:** Hold the dog while someone pets; force hand-sniff rituals; punish growling and call it training (`book-research-defra-002-avoid-harsh`).

### Train the dog

- **Know:** Guests can predict pressure or predict calm — your setup decides.  
- **Do:** Learn that staying back or looking at you can pay; experience guests as **optional**, not mandatory assault.  
- **Don't:** Rehearse full-contact greetings while over threshold; learn that growling is ignored until snap.

**Dogs Trust-style progression (when the dog is sub-threshold):**

1. **Desensitise knock/bell** — quiet table knock, then louder; then real door with helper; reward calm (`book-research-dt-visitor-bed-knock`).  
2. **Bed cue** — knock means go to bed for long-lasting chew; guest enters while dog settles; clear release word before approach.  
3. **Real guests** — same routine; optional meet if dog is soft; jump-up and barking sections on DT site if needed.

**Hard stop:** stiffening, growling, or unable to eat → management-only (gate, separate room, no guest contact). No flooding to “socialise.”

## Section 3 — Gentle handling for vet and groom prep

Handling is **choice-building**, not restraint drills. You are teaching “human hands predict brief, paid touches” — not “you will be grabbed until you submit.”

**Method sketch:** one body zone at a time; mark and pay for voluntary approach or stillness; seconds before minutes; stop while easy. Pair collar, paws, mouth, and harness with food at the dog’s pace. If they leave, they leave — do not chase.

### Train yourself

- **Know:** Forced handling teaches fight or freeze; vet day then costs more.  
- **Do:** Daily three-second touches where the dog is soft; build a “chin rest” or “paws on lap” only if the dog offers.  
- **Don't:** Hold down for nail clips while the dog panics; “dominate” through fear.

### Train the dog

- **Know:** Touch can predict treats or predict trap — your history decides.  
- **Do:** Learn that offering a paw or holding still for a second earns release and pay.  
- **Don't:** Learn that growling is punished (`book-research-defra-002-avoid-harsh`); escalate to bite.

Growling means **stop and reassess** — distance, pace, or professional help. Recommended review before publication for intensity on puppies vs adults (`editorial/qualified-review-plan.md`).

## Troubleshooting

- **If the dog is fine until the guest moves** → Motion trigger; increase distance; guest stillness; shorter visits.  
- **If the dog is worse on lead at the door** → Lead frustration or trapped feeling — try barrier greeting with dog off lead in secure room.  
- **If guests ignore your rules** → Do not open the door; management includes sending people to the pub.

## Stop if

- Growling, snapping, or attempts to bite — **stop** guest approach; secure dog; Chapter 14 and professional help.  
- Dog cannot eat or play at any distance with guest visible — threshold too high; guest leaves, try another day.  
- You are embarrassed into “just letting them say hi” — **stop**; safety over social nicety.
