# Calibration — humour with welfare guardrails

My dog once performed a perfect sit while a squirrel committed what I can only describe as a war crime against dignity on the fence. She did not break the sit. I, less trained, made a small undignified noise.

I tell you this because **proof in the garden is not proof at the park.** Dogs aren’t being difficult when the world gets interesting; the world got interesting. Our job is to shrink the world until success is likely again — not to win an argument with a fence full of squirrels.

If your dog’s ears pin back, they lean away, or they suddenly develop an intense fascination with a spot on the horizon that definitely isn’t you, **believe them.** Pause. Increase distance. Swap the lesson for something easy they already love.

The joke, if there is one, is on us — the humans who thought “sit” would travel through pure willpower like Wi‑Fi. It doesn’t. It travels on treats, timing, and a bit of self-respect on both sides.
