# Calibration — instructional tone

You’re standing in the kitchen with a pocket of slightly sad cheese and a dog who has learned that the fridge is a shrine. That’s fine. Training doesn’t require a spotless house or a dog who looks like a brochure.

Here’s the whole game in one loop: **make it easy, mark the moment, pay generously, reset before it gets weird.**

1. **Set the scene.** Quiet room. No doorbell. Phone on silent — future you will thank present you.  
2. **Ask once.** Give your cue in a normal voice, not a stadium announcement.  
3. **Mark the instant** the behaviour happens — your “yes” word or click — then deliver the treat within a second or two.  
4. **Release.** Let them sniff, shake off, or wander. You’re teaching a behaviour, not running a prison shift.  
5. **Stop while it’s still fun.** Three good reps beat twelve grumpy ones.

**Look for:** Eager approach, soft body, willing repeats.  
**Stop if:** They turn away, freeze, or start scanning for exits. Smaller steps, better rewards, or a break — not louder cues.

If you’re about to repeat the cue like a broken car alarm, try the owner mantra first: **make it easier — then ask again.** That’s you training the human.

That’s it. No mystique. Just clear cause and effect, with kindness built in.
